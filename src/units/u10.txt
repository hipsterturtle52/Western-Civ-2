=== LESSON
## The big picture
The **First World War (1914–1918)** destroyed four empires (German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman), killed about **ten million soldiers**, and ended a century of European confidence. It also produced the **Russian Revolution**, which created the world's first communist state. Together they are about one exam question in nine. Know the long-term causes, the chain of events in July 1914, why the war became a stalemate, how "total war" changed society, what the peace treaties did, and the steps from the tsar's fall to Lenin's rule.

## Long-term causes: M-A-I-N
A handy memory aid is **MAIN**: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism.
- **Militarism:** huge conscript armies, detailed mobilization timetables, and an **arms race**, above all the **Anglo-German naval race** after Britain launched the revolutionary battleship **HMS Dreadnought (1906)**. Germany's **Schlieffen Plan** assumed a two-front war and called for knocking out France quickly through neutral **Belgium** before turning on Russia.
- **Alliances:** Europe was split into the **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) and the **Triple Entente** (France, Russia, Britain). A local quarrel could pull in every great power.
- **Imperialism:** colonial rivalries, especially the two **Moroccan Crises** (1905 and 1911), in which German pressure on France only drove Britain and France closer together.
- **Nationalism:** French desire to recover Alsace-Lorraine; **Pan-Slavism**, Russia's claim to protect fellow Slavs; and, most dangerous, **Serbian nationalism**, which aimed to unite the South Slavs, many of whom lived inside Austria-Hungary.

The **Balkans** were the "powder keg of Europe." In the **Bosnian Crisis (1908)** Austria-Hungary **annexed Bosnia-Herzegovina**, home to many Serbs, infuriating Serbia and humiliating Russia, which backed down. In the **Balkan Wars (1912–1913)**, Serbia nearly doubled its territory at Ottoman expense. Austria-Hungary came to see Serbia as a mortal threat.

## The July Crisis of 1914
-> A Bosnian Serb nationalist assassinates Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo (June 28, 1914) | Austria, backed by Germany's "blank check," issues an ultimatum and declares war on Serbia; alliances and mobilization plans take over | Within a week Germany, Russia, France and Britain are at war

On **June 28, 1914**, **Gavrilo Princip**, a young Bosnian Serb linked to the Serbian secret society the **Black Hand**, shot **Archduke Franz Ferdinand**, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife in **Sarajevo**. Austria-Hungary decided to crush Serbia. Germany gave it unconditional support, the "**blank check**" (July 5–6). Austria sent Serbia a harsh **ultimatum** (July 23); Serbia accepted most of it, but Austria declared war on **July 28**. Russia mobilized to protect Serbia. Germany declared war on **Russia (August 1)** and **France (August 3)** and, following the Schlieffen Plan, invaded **Belgium**. Because Britain had guaranteed Belgian neutrality (Treaty of London, 1839), **Britain declared war on Germany on August 4**. Italy stayed neutral, then joined the Allies in 1915 in return for promised Austrian land. The **Ottoman Empire** (1914) and **Bulgaria** (1915) joined Germany and Austria-Hungary as the **Central Powers**.

Who was to blame? The Allies said Germany, and wrote "war guilt" into the peace treaty. In the 1960s the German historian **Fritz Fischer** argued that Germany's leaders had aggressive aims and welcomed war. Others stress shared responsibility: rigid alliances and mobilization plans, nationalism everywhere, and leaders who "sleepwalked" into a war they did not fully foresee.

## The war: stalemate and total war
**Western Front.** The Germans swept through Belgium toward Paris, but the French and British stopped them at the **First Battle of the Marne (September 1914)**. Both sides dug in. Soon a line of **trenches** ran 400 miles from the North Sea to Switzerland. Defensive weapons, **machine guns**, barbed wire and artillery, made attacks suicidal; offensives gained a few miles at the cost of hundreds of thousands of lives. At **Verdun (1916)** the Germans tried to "bleed France white"; the battle lasted ten months and cost about 700,000 casualties. At the **Somme (1916)** the British suffered about 57,000 casualties on the first day alone; **tanks** made their debut there. **Poison gas** was first used on a large scale at Ypres in 1915. Airplanes were used for scouting and then combat.

**Eastern Front and elsewhere.** The Germans under **Hindenburg and Ludendorff** crushed a Russian invasion at **Tannenberg (1914)**. The front in the east stayed more mobile, but Russia suffered staggering losses. To open a supply route to Russia, the Allies attacked the Ottoman Straits at **Gallipoli (1915–1916)**, a costly failure. The British encouraged an **Arab Revolt** against the Ottomans (with T. E. Lawrence). In 1915 the Ottoman government carried out the **Armenian genocide**, killing over a million Armenians.

**At sea.** Britain's **naval blockade** slowly starved Germany. Germany replied with **submarine (U-boat)** warfare; the sinking of the liner ***Lusitania* (1915)**, with 128 Americans aboard, angered the United States.

-> Germany resumes unrestricted submarine warfare (1917); the Zimmermann Telegram offers Mexico U.S. land | The United States enters the war (April 1917) | Fresh American troops and resources tip the balance after Russia leaves the war

In 1917 Germany gambled on **unrestricted submarine warfare**, and the British intercepted the **Zimmermann Telegram**, a German proposal of alliance to Mexico. The **United States declared war in April 1917**. That same year Russia collapsed into revolution and left the war (**Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, March 1918**). Germany shifted troops west for a final **Spring Offensive (1918)**, which failed. Allied counterattacks, with growing American forces, pushed the exhausted Germans back. With its allies collapsing and revolution spreading at home, Germany's Kaiser **William II abdicated**, and an **armistice** took effect on **November 11, 1918**.

## The home front: total war
The war was **total war**: entire societies and economies were mobilized. Governments took control of industry, labor, food and prices; in Germany, Walther Rathenau's War Raw Materials Office directed the economy, and the 1916 Auxiliary Service Law drafted civilian men for war work. Food and fuel were **rationed**. **Propaganda** demonized the enemy, and censorship (such as Britain's Defence of the Realm Act) controlled the news. Governments paid with borrowing and printing money, causing **inflation** and leaving huge **debts**, much of it owed to the **United States**, which became the world's leading creditor.

Millions of **women** took jobs in munitions factories, offices, transport and nursing. Partly in recognition, **British women over 30 gained the vote in 1918**, and women in Germany, Austria, Poland and other new republics won it in 1918–1919 (France waited until 1944). The war killed around **10 million soldiers** and millions of civilians; a "**lost generation**" of young men was gone. The **influenza pandemic** of 1918–1919 killed even more people worldwide. Disillusionment with old values filled postwar literature, such as Erich Maria Remarque's ***All Quiet on the Western Front*** (1929).

## The peace settlements
-> Germany asks for an armistice expecting Wilson's Fourteen Points | Paris Peace Conference (1919): Treaty of Versailles with war guilt, reparations, disarmament and territorial losses | New nation-states in central Europe; a resentful Germany; a League of Nations without the U.S.

The victors met in Paris in **1919**. The "**Big Four**" were **Woodrow Wilson** (United States), **David Lloyd George** (Britain), **Georges Clemenceau** (France) and **Vittorio Orlando** (Italy). Germany and Soviet Russia were not invited to negotiate. Wilson's **Fourteen Points** (January 1918) called for open diplomacy, freedom of the seas, free trade, arms reduction, **national self-determination**, and a **League of Nations** to keep the peace. Clemenceau, whose country had been invaded twice in fifty years, wanted security and German payment. Lloyd George sought a middle way.

The **Treaty of Versailles** (signed **June 28, 1919**) with Germany:
- **Article 231, the "war guilt clause,"** made Germany accept responsibility for the war's losses, the legal basis for **reparations** (set in 1921 at 132 billion gold marks).
- **Territory:** Alsace-Lorraine to France; land in the east to the new **Poland**, including the "**Polish Corridor**" to the sea that split East Prussia from Germany; **Danzig** a free city; the **Saar** under League control for 15 years; all **colonies** taken and handed to the victors as League **mandates**.
- **Military:** army limited to **100,000** men; no air force, tanks or submarines; the **Rhineland demilitarized** and occupied by the Allies.
- Union (**Anschluss**) with Austria forbidden.

Germans called it a *Diktat*, a dictated peace. The economist **John Maynard Keynes** warned in *The Economic Consequences of the Peace* (1919) that the reparations would wreck Europe's economy. Separate treaties broke up Austria-Hungary (**Saint-Germain** with Austria, **Trianon** with Hungary, which lost two-thirds of its territory) and punished Bulgaria (Neuilly) and the Ottoman Empire (**Sèvres**, 1920, later replaced by the Treaty of **Lausanne**, 1923, after Mustafa Kemal's Turkish nationalists fought back). New or restored states appeared: **Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia** (the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes), Austria, Hungary, Finland, Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania. But "self-determination" was applied unevenly, leaving large minorities, such as 3 million Germans in Czechoslovakia's **Sudetenland**. Former Ottoman Arab lands became British (Palestine, Iraq, Transjordan) and French (Syria, Lebanon) **mandates**, despite wartime promises to the Arabs and Britain's **Balfour Declaration (1917)** supporting a Jewish "national home" in Palestine.

The **League of Nations** was created, but the **U.S. Senate refused to ratify the treaty**, so the United States never joined, a fatal blow to the League's authority.

## The Russian Revolution
**Background.** Nicholas II's Russia entered the war already strained by the 1905 revolution, a weak Duma, peasant land hunger and a small but radical industrial working class. The war was a disaster: millions of casualties, shortages of weapons and food, and inflation. Nicholas took personal command at the front in 1915, leaving the government to the unpopular Empress Alexandra and the mystic **Rasputin**, who was murdered by nobles in 1916.

**The February Revolution (March 1917).** Bread riots and strikes in the capital, **Petrograd**, turned into revolution when soldiers refused to fire on crowds and joined them. **Nicholas II abdicated**, ending three centuries of Romanov rule. Power was shared in an unstable "**dual power**": the **Provisional Government**, dominated by liberals and later led by the moderate socialist **Alexander Kerensky**, and the **Petrograd Soviet** (council) of workers' and soldiers' deputies. The Provisional Government's fatal mistake was to **keep fighting the war** and postpone land reform until an elected assembly could meet.

-> The Provisional Government keeps fighting an unpopular war and delays land reform | Lenin's Bolsheviks promise "Peace, Land, Bread" and seize power in the October Revolution (November 1917) | Bolshevik dictatorship, withdrawal from the war (Brest-Litovsk), civil war

**Lenin and the Bolsheviks.** **Vladimir Lenin** led the **Bolsheviks**, the faction of Russia's Marxist party that split from the more moderate **Mensheviks** in **1903**. Lenin argued that a small, disciplined **vanguard party** of professional revolutionaries must lead the workers, and that Russia could leap to socialism without a long capitalist stage. The Germans, hoping to knock Russia out of the war, let him travel home from Switzerland in a sealed train in April 1917. His **April Theses** demanded "**All power to the Soviets**," and his slogan "**Peace, Land, and Bread**" spoke directly to soldiers, peasants and workers. After a failed rising in July and a failed right-wing coup by General Kornilov, the Bolsheviks won majorities in the soviets.

**The October Revolution.** On **October 25, 1917, by the old Russian calendar (November 7 in the Western calendar)**, Bolshevik forces organized by **Leon Trotsky** seized key points in Petrograd and the **Winter Palace**, where the Provisional Government met. Lenin's government decreed peace, gave land to the peasants and put factories under workers' control. When elections to a **Constituent Assembly** gave the Bolsheviks only about a quarter of the votes (the peasant-based Socialist Revolutionaries won more), Lenin **dissolved the Assembly** in January 1918. He created a secret police, the **Cheka**.

**Brest-Litovsk and civil war.** In the **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 1918)** Russia gave up Poland, the Baltic lands, Finland and **Ukraine**, about a third of its European population. A brutal **Russian Civil War (1918–1921)** followed between the Bolshevik **Reds** and the anti-Bolshevik **Whites** (monarchists, liberals and others), who received limited help from Allied troops. Trotsky built the disciplined **Red Army**. The Bolsheviks shot the **tsar and his family** (July 1918). They won because they held the central heartland and railways, were united and ruthless, and the Whites were divided and associated with the return of landlords.

To feed the Red Army, Lenin imposed **War Communism**: nationalization of industry and forced **requisitioning of grain** from peasants. The economy collapsed and famine followed. When sailors at the **Kronstadt** naval base, once Bolshevik heroes, rebelled in 1921, Lenin retreated to the **New Economic Policy (NEP)**, which let peasants sell surplus grain and allowed small private businesses, while the state kept the "commanding heights" (banks, heavy industry, foreign trade). In **1922** the state became the **Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR)**. The Communist International (**Comintern**, 1919) promoted revolution abroad. Lenin died in **1924**, setting off a power struggle between Trotsky and **Joseph Stalin** (Unit 11).
