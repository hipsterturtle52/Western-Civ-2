=== LESSON
## The big picture
The Congress of Vienna tried to freeze Europe in 1815. It could not stop the ideas released by the French and Industrial Revolutions. Between 1815 and 1848 Europeans invented the "**isms**" that still frame politics: **conservatism, liberalism, nationalism and socialism**. In culture, **Romanticism** rebelled against Enlightenment reason. And waves of revolution, in the 1820s, in 1830 and above all in **1848**, tested whether the old order could hold. A typical exam question gives you a quotation and asks which ideology it expresses, so focus on what each "ism" believed and who its spokesmen were.

## The four ideologies
**Conservatism** defended the old order of monarchy, aristocracy, established church and tradition. Following **Edmund Burke**, conservatives argued that society is a living organism built over centuries; change should be slow and organic, never imposed by abstract theories like "the rights of man." The French writer **Joseph de Maistre** went further, defending absolute monarchy and the pope. In practice, conservatism meant **Metternich**, who saw liberalism and nationalism as diseases that would tear apart his multinational Austrian Empire.

**Liberalism** was the creed of the middle class. Liberals wanted **constitutions**, representative government, **equality before the law**, freedom of the press, speech and religion, and **laissez-faire** economics (Adam Smith, Ricardo). But most early liberals did **not** want democracy: they favored voting limited to men of **property** and education. The English philosopher **Jeremy Bentham** taught **utilitarianism**: laws should be judged by whether they produce "the greatest happiness of the greatest number." His follower **John Stuart Mill** later defended individual freedom in *On Liberty* (1859) and argued for women's rights.

**Nationalism** held that humanity is naturally divided into **nations**, peoples sharing a language, culture and history, and that each nation should have its own state. The German thinker **Johann Gottfried Herder** taught that each people has a unique spirit (*Volksgeist*) expressed in its language and folk songs. **Giuseppe Mazzini** founded **Young Italy** (1831) to unite Italy as a democratic republic. Nationalism was revolutionary in 1815 because it threatened multinational empires (Austria, Russia, the Ottoman Empire) and divided lands (Germany and Italy, each split into many states).

**Socialism** responded to the misery of industrial workers. Its basic idea: private ownership and competition cause inequality, so society should own or cooperatively manage the means of production. The early **utopian socialists** hoped to persuade society by example. **Robert Owen**, a British mill owner, built a model factory community at **New Lanark** with schools and decent housing, and later a commune at New Harmony, Indiana. **Charles Fourier** designed cooperative communities called *phalanxes*. **Henri de Saint-Simon** wanted society run by experts and industrialists for the good of all. **Louis Blanc** called for state-funded "social workshops" guaranteeing the **right to work**. **Pierre-Joseph Proudhon** declared "**Property is theft!**" and became a father of **anarchism**.

-> Industrial capitalism creates a large, impoverished working class | Marx and Engels publish The Communist Manifesto (1848) | "Scientific" socialism: history is class struggle and capitalism will be overthrown by the proletariat

**Karl Marx** and **Friedrich Engels** dismissed the utopians as naive and called their own theory **scientific socialism**. *The Communist Manifesto* (February 1848) begins, "A specter is haunting Europe, the specter of communism," and ends, "Workers of the world, unite!" Its argument: "The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of **class struggles**." Economic forces drive history (**historical materialism**); under capitalism the **bourgeoisie** (owners) exploits the **proletariat** (workers), who will become a majority, overthrow capitalism in a revolution, set up a dictatorship of the proletariat, and eventually create a classless, stateless communist society. Marx borrowed the idea of history moving through conflicts (the **dialectic**) from the German philosopher **G. W. F. Hegel**. Marx's influence came mostly after 1848 (Units 8 and 9).

## Romanticism
**Romanticism** (about 1790–1850) was a cultural revolt against the Enlightenment's cool rationalism and against the ugliness of industrial cities. Romantics prized **emotion, intuition and imagination** over reason; **nature** as sublime and spiritual; the **individual genius** and rebel; the **medieval past** (the Gothic revival); and the folk culture of each people, which tied Romanticism closely to **nationalism**.

Key figures: in poetry, **William Wordsworth** and Samuel Taylor Coleridge (*Lyrical Ballads*, 1798) celebrated nature and common speech; **Lord Byron** lived and died (1824) as a Romantic rebel fighting for Greek independence. In Germany, **Goethe** wrote *The Sorrows of Young Werther* and *Faust*, and the **Brothers Grimm** collected German folk tales. **Victor Hugo** (*The Hunchback of Notre-Dame*, 1831) and Sir **Walter Scott** (*Ivanhoe*) turned to the Middle Ages. In painting, **Caspar David Friedrich** showed lonely figures before vast landscapes, and **Eugène Delacroix** painted ***Liberty Leading the People*** (1830). In music, **Beethoven** bridged classicism and Romanticism, and **Chopin** wove Polish folk dances into his piano works. Romanticism also fed a **religious revival** after the skepticism of the Enlightenment.

## Metternich's system and the revolts of the 1820s
Metternich used the **Concert of Europe** to crush revolutions. When liberal revolts broke out in **Spain** and **Naples** (1820), the conservative powers declared at the Congress of **Troppau** (1820) a right to intervene; Austrian troops crushed the Naples revolt (1821) and a French army restored the Spanish king (1823). Britain refused to join these interventions and drifted away from the Concert. In the German Confederation, after a nationalist student murdered a conservative writer, Metternich pushed through the **Carlsbad Decrees (1819)**, which dissolved student nationalist societies (*Burschenschaften*), censored the press and put university professors under surveillance.

Two exceptions broke the pattern:
- **Greek War of Independence (1821–1829).** Greeks revolted against the Ottoman Empire. European Romantics (like Byron) adored the cause of ancient Greece's descendants, and Britain, France and Russia intervened, destroying the Ottoman-Egyptian fleet at **Navarino (1827)**. Greece became independent (recognized 1830–1832). Nationalism had won, with great-power help, because the rebels were Christians fighting the Muslim Ottomans and Russia saw a chance to weaken the Ottomans.
- In Russia, the **Decembrist Revolt (December 1825)**: liberal army officers who had seen Western Europe during the Napoleonic wars rebelled for a constitution when Nicholas I took the throne. They were crushed. **Nicholas I** (r. 1825–1855) became the "gendarme of Europe," ruling under the slogan "**Orthodoxy, Autocracy, Nationality**."

## The revolutions of 1830
In France, the restored Bourbon **Louis XVIII** had accepted a **Charter (1814)** with a parliament elected by a tiny wealthy electorate. His brother **Charles X** (r. 1824–1830) was an ultra-royalist. When he issued the **July Ordinances** (1830), dissolving the chamber, restricting the vote further and censoring the press, Paris rose in the **July Revolution**. Charles fled, and liberals installed his cousin **Louis-Philippe** of Orléans as the "**citizen king**" of the **July Monarchy** (1830–1848). The electorate roughly doubled but stayed small; the regime favored the wealthy bourgeoisie (his minister Guizot told those who wanted the vote to "get rich").

The French example spread. **Belgium** revolted against Dutch rule in 1830 and won independence, guaranteed as permanently **neutral** by the great powers (1839). The **Polish November Uprising** (1830–31) against Russian rule was crushed, and Nicholas I abolished Poland's constitution. Revolts in Italy were put down by Austria.

## The revolutions of 1848: the "springtime of peoples"
-> Bad harvests and the potato blight (1845–47), unemployment and food prices; liberal and nationalist frustration | Revolutions in Paris, Vienna, Berlin, Milan, Budapest, Prague and Rome in 1848 | Most fail by 1849, divided by class and nationality and crushed by royal armies; but serfdom ends in Austria

**France.** In **February 1848**, banquets for electoral reform turned into revolution. Louis-Philippe abdicated, and the **Second Republic** proclaimed **universal male suffrage** and created **National Workshops** to employ the jobless (inspired by Louis Blanc). The newly elected, mostly rural and conservative assembly closed the workshops; Paris workers rose in the **June Days** and were crushed by the army, with thousands killed. Frightened voters elected **Louis Napoleon Bonaparte**, nephew of the emperor, as president in December 1848. He overthrew the republic in 1851 and became **Emperor Napoleon III** in 1852.

**Austrian Empire.** In **March 1848**, a revolt in Vienna forced **Metternich** to flee. Hungarians under **Lajos Kossuth** won self-government; Czechs in Prague demanded rights; Italians in Lombardy-Venetia revolted. The Austrian assembly **abolished serfdom** (1848), the one lasting gain. Then the counterrevolution struck: General Windischgrätz bombarded Prague (June) and retook Vienna (October); the young **Franz Joseph** became emperor (December 1848); and in 1849 **Russian troops** helped crush Hungary. The nationalities had fought each other as much as the emperor; the Croats, for example, helped Austria against Hungary.

**Germany.** Liberals elected the **Frankfurt Parliament** (1848–49) to unite Germany under a constitution. It argued over whether Germany should include Austria ("greater Germany") or exclude it ("smaller Germany"), and finally offered the crown of a smaller Germany to King **Frederick William IV of Prussia** (1849). He refused a "crown from the gutter" offered by a parliament, and Prussian troops dispersed the remaining radicals. German unity would come by force under Bismarck instead (Unit 8).

**Italy.** Revolts spread from Sicily to Milan and Venice. King **Charles Albert of Piedmont-Sardinia** declared war on Austria but was defeated at Custoza (1848) and Novara (1849). In Rome, **Mazzini** and **Garibaldi** led a **Roman Republic** (1849) until French troops restored the pope. Only Piedmont kept its new constitution (the Statuto).

**Why 1848 failed:** liberals feared the radical workers more than the kings; nationalities clashed with each other; peasants lost interest once serfdom was abolished; and the armies stayed loyal to the monarchs. Afterward, idealism gave way to **realism**: Realpolitik, the politics of power rather than principles, would unify Italy and Germany.

=== MUSTKNOW
- **Conservatism** (Burke, de Maistre, Metternich): tradition, monarchy, church, gradual change.
- **Liberalism:** constitutions, rights, free press, laissez-faire, but votes for property owners only. Bentham's **utilitarianism**; J. S. Mill.
- **Nationalism:** each people (language, culture, history) deserves its own state. Herder's Volksgeist; **Mazzini's Young Italy**.
- **Utopian socialists:** Owen (New Lanark), Fourier (phalanxes), Saint-Simon, Louis Blanc (right to work); **Proudhon** ("Property is theft," anarchism).
- **Communist Manifesto (1848):** class struggle, bourgeoisie vs. proletariat, historical materialism, inevitable revolution.
- **Romanticism:** emotion, nature, the past, folk culture, genius. Wordsworth, Byron, Goethe, Grimm, Hugo, Friedrich, Delacroix.
- **Metternich's tools:** Carlsbad Decrees (1819), intervention in Naples and Spain (Troppau, 1820).
- **Greek independence** (1821–30) succeeded with British, French and Russian help; the **Decembrist Revolt** (1825) failed.
- **1830:** July Revolution → Louis-Philippe; Belgian independence; Polish revolt crushed.
- **1848:** February Revolution → Second Republic → June Days → Louis Napoleon. Metternich falls; **serfdom abolished in Austria**; Frankfurt Parliament fails (Frederick William IV refuses crown); Russia crushes Hungary.
